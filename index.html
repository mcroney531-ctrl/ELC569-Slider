Persona QA

Persona QA Testing Tool

Test your experience from more than one point of view.

Use AI-generated test personas to explore how different people, situations, and behaviors might expose issues in something you've built, before you put it in front of real users.

This tool helps you create a small panel of simulated users, change the conditions they're testing under, and take those personas into your build for structured exploratory testing.

These are simulated test personas. They can broaden what you think to test, but they do not replace usability testing or research with real people.

Step 1 of 4

Why persona testing?

It's easy to test a build the way you expect someone to use it. Persona testing gives you a reason to approach the same experience differently.

Instead of only asking "Does this work?", you can also ask:

  • Does this still work for someone who is new to the topic?
  • What happens when someone is in a hurry?
  • What if their situation doesn't fit the path I designed?

A good test persona gives you a specific point of view to adopt while testing. That can help uncover unclear instructions, missing paths, assumptions, confusing interactions, weak recovery states, and situations you may not have thought to try.

The goal isn't to predict what every real user will do. The goal is to expand the range of things you deliberately test.

Step 2 of 4

Why AI can help

AI helps you test more kinds of situations and perspectives without inventing every persona yourself. Useful dimensions include:

  • Experience with the task
  • Familiarity with the subject
  • Confidence
  • Time and attention
  • Working habits
  • Device or situational constraints
  • Unusual but realistic circumstances

AI generates possibilities to test, not evidence about your users.

A generated persona can help expose a real problem in the design, but the persona itself doesn't prove that real users have that problem. Use it alongside real-user research and testing, not instead of it.

Step 4 of 4

Key terms

Persona
A simulated user you temporarily adopt while testing. Each persona has a stable goal, starting point, working style and perspective.
Test panel
The group of personas you're currently testing with.
Coverage lens
The reason a persona is included. A newcomer tests assumptions about prior knowledge; a time-constrained user tests what happens when attention is limited.
Typical
No added challenge. The persona behaves according to their normal working style.
Rushed
The same persona is under time pressure and cuts corners compared with how they normally behave.
Inconsistent
The persona contradicts something they said, selected, answered or did earlier in the same test pass. Example: they say they want email updates, then later select "Do not contact me."
Edge case
A real but less-common circumstance the experience may not have been designed around. Example: a volunteer form assumes everyone has a fixed weekly schedule, but this persona's availability changes every week. An edge case isn't bizarre behavior added just to break the design; it's a legitimate situation the design may not cover well.
Key moment
An important checkpoint worth testing, such as choosing a plan, submitting a form, receiving feedback or recovering from an error.
Journey
The route the persona takes through those key moments.

Worked example

Priya is an experienced employee completing a benefits form.

Typical
She already knows what she wants and moves efficiently through familiar fields.
Rushed
She has five minutes before a meeting and starts accepting defaults without checking them.
Inconsistent
She selects employee-only coverage, then later adds a dependent.
Edge case
Her current plan no longer appears in the available options.
Journey: One step back
She reaches Review, notices something wrong, returns to plan selection, then continues.

Priya stays Priya. The test condition changes around the same person.
